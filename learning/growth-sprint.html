The 12-Week Growth Sprint

The 12-month roadmap didn't survive contact with real life — a month is long enough to slide for weeks before you notice. This is the replacement: a tighter loop, four threads running every single week instead of taking turns, and a real thing shipped and presented at the end of the quarter.

Why weekly, why these four

The old plan had two failure modes: it was monthly, so a bad week was invisible until it was a bad month; and it was siloed — data engineering for six months, then AI, so nothing you cared about touched anything else. This one fixes both. Every week, in one sitting or a few short ones, you touch all four things you actually said you wanted:

  • Architecture — get comfortable naming and drawing systems, not just building them. A weekly rep, not a month locked in a room with a textbook.
  • Build — one real project, built with Claude, in territory outside your comfort zone. A slice a week, a shipped v1 by week 12.
  • Claude fluency — go past "I use Claude Code daily" into how it actually works: prompting, tool use, agents, evals, guardrails.
  • Presentation & inhibition — a graded exposure ladder, one rung most weeks: alone → one person → a small group → the real thing, on camera, in public.
Pick the project — before Week 1

Default candidate, if you want one ready-made: Pulse's Phase 3 — the three-column dashboard (have / changed / should-do) — has been sitting at not started. It's a real UI, a real audience of one (you), and it pushes you into frontend/product territory you don't already own. That's the shape this sprint is built for.

If something else is pulling at you harder, build that instead. The requirement isn't the subject — it's that the project has a UI or an output a non-technical person can react to, that Claude is a real collaborator on it (not just autocomplete), and that it's small enough to have a demoable v1 in 12 weeks.

How to use it
  1. Open the week. Four repcards: Architecture, Build, Claude, Presentation. Read all four before you start any of them — they're meant to inform each other.
  2. Do the reps in whatever order fits your week. None of them need a full day. The architecture rep is often 20 minutes. The presentation rep is often five.
  3. Learn the Claude thread by prompting, not reading. Paste the prompt cards into Claude, push back, ask for a diagram when words aren't enough.
  4. Don't skip the presentation rep. It's the one that's easiest to drop and the one the whole plan exists to fix. It's designed to feel slightly too easy each week — that's the point of a graded ladder.
  5. Tick the done-when list before you move to the next week. If you're behind, compress — don't skip a thread entirely.

Budget ~4–6 focused hours a week, spread across sessions. Lighter than the old plan on purpose — a plan you can actually keep beats one that looks impressive on paper.

Weeks 1–3

Lay the Foundation

Pick the project, name its architecture, get Claude wired in, get on camera for the first time.
WEEK 01
Kickoff & Commit
Pick the project, sketch current-state vs target-state, and record yourself pitching it — alone, for 90 seconds.
WEEK 02
Name the Architecture
Write a real ADR for your stack, scaffold the repo, wire in one live Claude call.
WEEK 03
First Vertical Slice
Ship one ugly, working screen with one real Claude-powered feature, then demo it live to one person.
Weeks 4–6

Build & Retro

Add memory, add initiative, then stop and look honestly at what's working.
WEEK 04
Make It Remember
Decide how state and persistence work, ship feature #2, demo to two people and actually take the critique.
WEEK 05
Give It Some Initiative
Add agentic, multi-step behavior; record your first on-camera walkthrough, just for yourself.
WEEK 06
Halfway Retro
No new feature — refactor, write your first evals, and watch weeks 1 and 5 back to back.
Weeks 7–9

Harden & Stretch

Take on the feature you've avoided, present live to a group, design under a clock.
WEEK 07
Harden It
A PII/security pass, the hardest feature on the list, and your first live demo to a small group.
WEEK 08
Make It Legible
Draw the real architecture diagram, polish the UI a stranger could use, and cede control mid-demo.
WEEK 09
Design Under Pressure
A timed, no-Claude scaling exercise, an integration that makes the build feel real, first recording sent to someone.
Weeks 10–12

Ship & Present

Deploy it, document it, rehearse it, then present the real thing.
WEEK 10
Ship It
Deploy somewhere real, write the 2am runbook, present the deployed thing to a wider audience.
WEEK 11
Rehearse the Capstone
Write the full architecture doc, have Claude grill you on it out loud, run a timed dry-run of the demo.
WEEK 12
Capstone
Ship v1, present live to the widest audience yet, retro the whole quarter, and set up the next one.
Where you land in 12 weeks

By week 12 you'll have one real thing shipped — built with Claude as an actual collaborator, not just an autocomplete — a one-page architecture doc you wrote and can defend out loud, and a presentation muscle that's been exercised ten times on a deliberately rising curve instead of never. That's a smaller-sounding outcome than "12 months, two capstones, a new job title" — and it's the one that's actually finishable, which makes it the one worth starting.
